Principles

What a client sees in the work

Each principle comes with the reason for it and what it looks like on a project.

  1. Start with the question

    Why
    The current estimate is often fit only for the previous question. Under-scope it and weak assumptions end up in the commercial case unnoticed; over-scope it and time and budget are spent before anyone knows what is needed.
    What you will see
    Every scope opens with the question being answered, who will rely on the result, and what would change if the number were different.

  2. Use what already exists

    Why
    Existing measurements, models, and reports usually hold more value than a new adviser’s first instinct suggests. Restarting good work adds cost and loses history.
    What you will see
    A review of existing data and analysis before new work is prescribed, with a plain statement of what is carried forward, what is challenged, and why.

  3. Match the method to the stage

    Why
    A layout comparison, a development-stage yield estimate, and a lender-reliance EYA are different tasks. Detailed assessment without the supporting project information creates false precision.
    What you will see
    Placeholders used deliberately and named as such, each with the stage by which a project-specific value must replace it.

  4. Agree the inputs before the run

    Why
    “Update the yield number” hides a long input list owned by several teams: layout revision, turbine and hub height, operating modes, data, constraints. Starting before it is agreed causes rework and several versions of the answer.
    What you will see
    A scenario summary agreed before each material iteration. A material change becomes the next iteration; it is not absorbed silently into the current one.

  5. Respect specialist boundaries

    Why
    Energy analysis is where many disciplines’ outputs meet, but it does not own them. Blurred responsibility is how errors survive review.
    What you will see
    Field deployment, environmental, electrical and grid, loads, and other formal sign-offs left with the appointed specialist. OBRAVEN defines the interface, challenges the input, and carries the consequence into the energy estimate.

  6. Work as part of the team

    Why
    What helps a project most is often outside the question that was asked: an input from another workstream that looks inconsistent, a choice that could be made differently. A consultant who stops at the edge of the scope does not mention it.
    What you will see
    Discussion as the work proceeds, as well as the document at the end. Anything that looks wrong for the project, or that could be done better, is raised with you, whether or not it is in the scope. What to do about it stays your decision.

  7. Keep senior judgement inside the analysis

    Why
    The judgement that matters – which assumption could change the answer, when a placeholder has done its job – is made inside the analysis itself.
    What you will see
    The person who discusses the question runs the analysis and signs the output.
Ways of working

Deliver, challenge, or lead the workstream

The roles OBRAVEN can take. Typical first assignments are on the home page.

Deliver

A defined piece of analysis at the depth the project needs now: wind resource assessment and EYA, layout optimisation, wake and blockage studies, 8760-hour production time-series.

Challenge

An independent look at consultant, seller, or OEM yield cases; loss factors, curtailment, and operating constraints; acquisition, financing, or repowering assumptions.

Lead the workstream

A defined energy analysis workstream inside the client or partner team: measurement strategy and procurement, the project-stage analytical roadmap, and coordination of consultants, data, and inputs.

How an engagement runs

From first call to close-out

The same sequence at any scale.

  1. Scoping call

    The question, intended use, data available, and timing. It ends with a defined next step or a clear no.

  2. Proposal

    Scope, outputs, fee basis, reliance, and assumptions on a few pages. NDA first where material is confidential.

  3. Appointment

    Any supplier onboarding and approvals the client requires are completed. Work begins once scope, terms, and authority to proceed are agreed in a signed engagement agreement or an accepted purchase order.

  4. Mobilisation

    A data request and an evidence and questions register. The first iteration starts once the inputs it needs are in. Starting on a partial set is possible, but it invites rework, so it is done only where that risk is clearly low.

  5. Visible iterations

    Working results shared as they mature, each stating the inputs it was run on.

  6. Output and close-out

    A decision-ready output, a close-out discussion, and a recommended next step. Anything noticed along the way that the project could do better is said, in scope or not.

Quality and traceability

What sits behind an OBRAVEN output

Not every iteration needs a full report. Every material iteration needs a controlled basis.

Reports and notes travel – to investment committees, lenders’ advisers, buyers, and the next analyst. A later reader should be able to identify the inputs, understand why the result changed, and – with the same data, models, and licences – rerun the analysis. Each material OBRAVEN iteration records:

  1. Question and project stage
  2. Data sources, versions, and long-term basis
  3. Layout, turbine, hub height, and operating mode
  4. Wind flow, wake, blockage, and neighbouring project treatment
  5. Loss factors, curtailment, and operating constraints
  6. Uncertainty, sensitivities, and provisional inputs
  7. Change from the previous iteration, open items, and owners
  8. Methods, standards, and reasons for any deviations

Before the work

The question, intended use, named audience, data basis, and the responsibility OBRAVEN is taking are agreed in writing.

During

Method choices, material assumptions, changes, limitations, and proportionate checks stay visible. Any separate review or specialist input is named with its role and boundary.

At the end

The output states what was assessed, what was checked, what remains uncertain, and what should happen next.

This describes a controlled founder-led practice. It does not imply a permanent in-house QA team, universal independent checking, or lender acceptance of every report. Relevant supplier relationships, commercial interests, and prior involvement are declared before independent challenge or procurement advice is agreed.

Routes

Appointed directly, or inside a wider team

Contracting and review arrangements differ by route. Clear scope, technical responsibility, specialist boundaries, and intended reliance are needed on every one.

Direct to the project

Developers, owners, investors, and project companies. OBRAVEN Limited contracts as the Irish company, works under NDA where needed, and issues OBRAVEN-branded outputs.

Inside a partner’s scope

Owner’s engineers, engineering, advisory, and due diligence firms that need a senior wind-analysis module. OBRAVEN supports the partner’s role and keeps its own boundaries visible.
