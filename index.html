Wind resource and energy yield analysis for decisions that shape project value and bankability.

OBRAVEN helps developers, owners, and investors decide what to measure, what to trust, and whether the yield number is ready for the next project gate – onshore and offshore, in Ireland and internationally. The starting point is the decision the project needs to support. Where financing is ahead, that means working backwards from the evidence a lender will expect.

When OBRAVEN is useful

The situations OBRAVEN is built for

Onshore or offshore, in development, acquisition, or financing.

A measurement campaign is about to be committed

Before equipment is locked in: are new measurements needed at all, and what must the data support?

The project has changed since its energy yield assessment

A new layout, turbine, or constraint. Which parts of the assessment still stand, and by how much could the yield change?

Two analyses give different answers

Separate differences in data, assumptions, and models from real differences in expected yield.

Layout or turbine choices are still open

Compare options on one consistent basis, with the energy cost of constraints and curtailment counted in: setbacks and exclusion zones, noise modes, shadow flicker, bat and bird regimes, grid limits. Focus on the scenarios that could change the ranking.

An inherited yield assessment is heading to financing

Test which of its data, assumptions, and results can be relied on, and which questions belong with the lender’s adviser, the turbine supplier, or the finance team.

A developer or investor is growing into wind

Sites are maturing faster than in-house wind expertise. OBRAVEN can own the energy analysis workstream end to end, as an in-house department would: the hands-on analysis, its tools and data, its planning and management, and the interface with every team around it, from engineering and procurement to finance and commercial. Its founder has led such a department.

Where OBRAVEN comes in

The finance-stage EYA may be iteration 50. It should build on the first 49.

The finance-stage energy yield assessment comes late. Measurement design, layout, turbine choice, losses, and uncertainty are worked through many times before it. Each iteration is a point where OBRAVEN can come in.

Why earlier matters

Some gaps cannot be fixed late

Measurement duration or height coverage found short at financing can rarely be fixed in the time left.

Missing data becomes uncertainty

A lower P90 relative to P50, and potentially less debt capacity.

Placeholders harden

Generic losses get challenged after they are already in the financial model.

History gets rebuilt

Without a controlled record, the formal assessment has to repeat work that has already been paid for.

At financing, OBRAVEN can prepare the independent assessment, or act on the owner’s side where the lender appoints its own adviser: prepare the brief, support the selection, and challenge the result.

What you can appoint OBRAVEN to do

Start with one question and one defined output.

Deliver the analysis, challenge an existing case, or lead a defined energy analysis workstream. A first assignment can extend into call-off senior support – one question at a time.

AssignmentQuestionOutput

  1. Energy yield assessment or update

    QuestionWhat is the current energy yield basis on the agreed data, layout, and technology?

    OutputEYA, update, or controlled results pack.

  2. Independent EYA review

    QuestionWhich assumptions or methods could materially change the number?

    OutputWritten findings, sensitivities, and questions to resolve.

  3. Layout, turbine, and wake study

    QuestionWhich configuration best balances energy value and the supplied constraints?

    OutputScenario comparison and recommended next scenarios.

  4. Measurement campaign design, procurement, or review

    QuestionWhat evidence is needed, and will the campaign deliver it?

    OutputCampaign strategy, specification, review note, or periodic data-quality report.

  5. Acquisition, financing, or repowering review

    QuestionCan the inherited assessment support the next decision, or should the plan change or stop?

    OutputFocused technical or due diligence note.

Or: an owner-side programme from evidence to financing

For projects heading to financing, three workstreams run as one programme and overlap, so measurement, analysis, and the formal assessment inform each other instead of running in sequence. The formal assessment can be OBRAVEN’s own, or another adviser’s with OBRAVEN on the owner’s side.

Why OBRAVEN

What you can expect from OBRAVEN

The alternatives are real: stretch the internal team, buy a broader consultancy scope, run more scenarios in software, or wait for financing. OBRAVEN is the right choice when the question needs senior wind analysis now, at the right depth.

What sits behind an OBRAVEN output

  1. The senior person does the work

    The person who scopes the question runs the analysis and stands behind the result. No hand-off down a delivery chain.

  2. From measurement to finance

    Measurement choices are made with the later EYA and lender review in view, by someone who has run met mast campaigns and led a global energy analysis group.

  3. The right depth for the stage

    No lender-style study when a screening answer will do. Placeholders are used deliberately and replaced when they stop being good enough.

  4. Independent of the case

    No turbines, equipment, software, or data to sell. Consultant, seller, and OEM yield cases can be challenged on their merits; supplier relationships are declared up front.

  5. A record that travels

    Every material iteration has a controlled basis, so the work survives handover, due diligence, and the next adviser.

Experience behind OBRAVEN

Stan Herasymenko founded OBRAVEN in 2026. His career began at ENERPARK in Poland, a firm that installed met masts and produced developers’ resource assessments, layouts, and energy yield assessments through the many iterations of a project’s development. There he measured wind, supervised 110+ met masts, ran that analysis, and went on to lead the wind analysis team. He continued in renewables advisory at DNV, and then at Mainstream Renewable Power, where he led around 20 specialists across five countries and built the offshore wind energy analysis capability across nine markets, from the US to South Korea and Australia.

At OBRAVEN that experience is applied directly, one project question at a time.
