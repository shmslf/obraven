Is dispatch-down in your P50?

The Irish grid turns down part of what a wind farm could produce. Where the yield report puts that loss decides whether it is counted once, twice or not at all.

By Stan Herasymenko, Founder & DirectorFirst published on LinkedIn (opens in a new tab), 2 November 2026Website edition, November 2026

In this piece
  1. Same words, different lines
  2. Sources
  3. Where this applies
  4. Questions to ask

The idea in brief

The problem

“Is dispatch-down in this number, in a sensitivity, or somewhere else?”

Why it happens

EirGrid and a yield report use some of the same words for different things, so the same lost energy can sit on different loss lines.

What to do

Before the P50 goes into the financial model, write down where dispatch-down is assessed, what each grid-related loss line holds, and whether it enters the central forecast.

On the island of Ireland, part of the energy a wind farm could produce gets turned down by the grid operator. EirGrid and SONI call it dispatch-down. In 2025 it was 11.4% of the available wind energy in Ireland, and 22.0% in Northern Ireland. For the first nine months of 2026, the figures were 13.8% and 24.3%.

How much there'll be in future is a question for grid specialists. Mine is simpler: where does it sit in the yield report?

EirGrid splits it in two. Constraint is local: the network in that area can't take the power, including during outages. Curtailment is system-wide: turning down any wind farm would help.

A yield report uses some of the same words for different things. Its curtailment line can also cover noise, wildlife, and wind sector management. And some outage-related constraints might also be counted under grid availability, depending on the report's loss definitions. So the same lost energy can be counted twice, or not at all.

In a recent study of wind farms, 31 of the 36 yield assessments with availability and curtailment estimates had 0% curtailment in their yield assessment, and many said outright that the grid stopping the plant wasn't considered.

That can be the right call, as long as it's clear where the loss is assessed – in a specialist study, a sensitivity, or the financial model – and whether it enters the central forecast.

So before the P50 goes into the model, I'd ask:

  • Is dispatch-down in this number, in a sensitivity, or somewhere else?
  • If it's a single loss line, what's inside it: constraint, curtailment, or both?
  • Is it a flat percentage, or does it change over the project's life?
  • Is any of it already in grid availability, or in another line?
  • If the P50 excludes dispatch-down, was that loss added back to the operational data before comparing?

Where this applies – and where it does not

Applies wherever a P50 from a yield report goes into a financial model for a wind farm on the island of Ireland: at financing, acquisition, or refinancing, and when operational data is compared with the original estimate. The same placement question applies in any market where the grid turns wind farms down. It does not say how much dispatch-down there will be in future, who is compensated, or what figure a project should assume; those are questions for grid specialists and the project's own studies. The percentages are EirGrid and SONI figures for a whole jurisdiction, dated in the text, and are not a project assumption.

Questions for your next project meeting

  1. Which loss definitions does our yield report use, and which line holds each grid-related loss?
  2. Is the dispatch-down figure we use a share of available energy, as EirGrid and SONI define it, and is it applied to the right point in the loss chain?
  3. How is dispatch-down treated in the P90, as well as in the P50?
  4. Where is the placement written down, so the yield and financial model teams read the same thing?
